Chains

A chain is a series of linked metal loops, worn on its own or used to carry a pendant. The style of the links decides how the chain looks, how it moves and how strong it is, and the lock decides how securely it stays on. Use this guide to compare popular chain types and lock options.

Chain Types

Here are some popular chain types:

Cable

A classic chain of uniform round or oval links, each interlocked with the next. Simple, strong and a safe choice for pendants.

Curb

The links are twisted so that they lie flat against the skin. A bold, everyday style that is popular in bracelets and men’s chains.

Figaro

A repeating pattern of short round links followed by one longer oval link, commonly three short and one long.

Rope

Small links twisted together into a spiral that looks like a rope. The twist catches the light and gives plenty of sparkle.

Box

Square links joined into a smooth, geometric chain. Sturdy and clean looking, and good for pendants and charms.

Snake

Tightly joined plates form a smooth, round and flexible chain that lies flat like snake skin.

Wheat

Oval links braided in a twisted pattern that resembles wheat stalks. Durable and flexible, and also known as Spiga or Palma.

Rolo

Round or oval links of equal size, connected one to the next. A simple, bold look that is easy to wear every day.

Bead / Ball

Small metal beads joined by short connectors at regular intervals. A light, classic style.

Herringbone

Flat, V-shaped segments linked tightly to form a smooth, ribbon-like chain with a mirror shine.

Choosing a Chain

Lighter links, such as cable and box, are a good match for pendants, while bolder links such as curb and rope stand out on their own. A larger clasp is easier to handle and usually more secure on heavier chains. To pick the right length, see the Necklace Size Guide.
